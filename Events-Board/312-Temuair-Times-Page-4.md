Column 2

---------------------------------------------------------------------------Is it a burden or an answered prayer? For many of us older Aislings, it may be a a blessing to catch the final item for half the price. To the younger Aislings it iss surely a grievous time to further oneself. The latest crash has followed the grand celebration of The Oren Ball. Grand Master Abusuave blames the crash on the ball, saying "The ball definitely caused this - people are after the newest garbs".
   It seems that not only has the worth of gold gone up, but item value has dropped. This has sent us into a mad spiral of depression, making the highs too high and the lows into a veritable abyss. Many Aislings have been forced to sell items into the flooded market. Grand Master Plexter proclaims "It seems so hard to even have enough gold for my repairs". 
Abusuave, when asked why he agrees to such low rates, complains "Lately I have had no other choice in order to stay in business".
   When followed to the root of our economy's fuctionality, Temuair can be described as "Trickle-down" economics. Abusuave explains "The largest portion of gold is brought by Chaos hunters selling their spoils". In short, the treasures of deep Chaos have governed cash flow from the more wealthy of Aislings which eventually filters down to the rest of Temuair.
   When Rucesion Official Mikau was questioned about the ball's effect on the market he replied "[It has] not really [affected the market]". In fact, officials claim that the crash has had no uncommon effect whatsoever on city crime and that things will return to normal in the near future. Aislings concur that this plague will indeed balance out eventually. 
   For now, this fair merchant is going to wait out this inbalance until prices match true item worth again.
  --Lord JaceKaos
