# The Mask

_by Rhaxephon in **Dark Ages**_

_Mileth Tavern: Deoch 48, 4th Moon, 23rd Sun, 4 a.m._

## Preface

I would like to tell a story that a dear friend of mine told me long, long
time ago. It's about someone who is very dear to many of us. This friend
of mine had left this land of Temuair, so I don't know what the outcome of
revealing this story would be. May Glioca bless us all.

* * *

_It's that mumbling voice again._

He was on his way back to the inn at nightfall. It's been a tiring day, and
he was all weighted down. When he crossed the town gate, he slowed down a
bit. The square outside Glioca Temple was clustered with people. People
started to enter the temple, with all solemn look on their faces. He
wondered. _Whose mass is it this time? Anglic's? Guardian's?_ But he
quickly shrugged off the thought and hastened his steps home. The voice of
the prayers, forming indistinct and lingering sound, started to annoy him.
He forgot when the last time he went to church was.

He finally stopped in front of an inn, where the sign at its front says:

> "**_Riona – Inn of Mileth. A soft bed and a pleasant dream_**  
> **_to any passing mundane or wandering Aisling._**"

He took a deep breath, and opened the door. There were few Aislings
standing idly, but no one bothered to look at him. Then he saw Riona
standing behind the counter, as usual. As she raised her eyes she saw him
immediately and smiled genially. He silently sunk into one of the chairs.

"Hey there. You look exhausted. Must be a rough day?"

"Yup, this place feels more pleasing than ever." He laughed dryly.

"So," she looked at him attentively and expectantly "How fared you today?"

He evaded her gaze and tried to act casually. "O not bad, sold a few
clothes and earned a few coins."

"See I told you things will get better." She grinned dearly. "I prayed the
Deity be with you today."

_But I didn't sell any clothes! Not one!_ He gave her a weak smile and
said nothing instead. _I am sorry Riona. You just don't understand how hard
it is on the other side of that counter…_ She looked so encouraging and
happy for him that he didn't want to spoil it. And he was just too tired to
extend this subject anyway.

He took a long look at Riona. Her hair was actually grayer than it
appeared. There were numerous times when she would mention her desire to
seclude in the west. He asked Riona why she hasn't, but she just shook her
head.

A minute later, their conversation was interrupted by a squeaking sound of
the door. A peasant boy came in, wearing some tattered peasant clothes.
The boy glanced round the lobby, and then picked a chair to sit down. The
first impression he got off the boy was _bad news_.

The boy looked very young, and had an air of haughty aloofness. He has seen
this type all too often, mostly on the street harassing or causing
troubles. When he looked at the boy, he realized the boy was hastily
avoiding eye contact. He started to ponder what this kid was doing here.

He turned back to Riona. She was engrossed in her mirror and didn't seem to
sense anything wrong. He sighed. _It's probably nothing._ People come in
and out of this place everyday. _I am just tired_. He wanted to head
upstairs and called it a day.

He noticed the boy had placed his right hand in his pocket. Maintaining the
sly expression on his face, the boy at times looked at Riona's counter. He
was then almost sure that the boy had a weapon hidden, and waited for the
right moment. There were less and less people in the lobby. He decided to
stick around for a while, in case any danger happened to Riona.

Time passed by. Just when he decided to talk to Riona, an Aisling showed up
in the lobby, bringing them some news that happened moment ago in Abel.
Apparently this messenger just came back from Abel, and there was a robbery
in Abel bank. An armed and masked unidentified suspect entered Abel bank
and threatened the clerk Lamont to give him all the money. Just as soon as
Lamont put every coin in a bag and ready to hand it to him, the suspect
seemed to hesitate, then he suddenly turned and dashed out of the bank.

This strange news seemed to have caught the boy's attention. When the
informer left, the boy sneered cynically.

"Silly clown." The boy muttered unwittingly.

He was stunned for a second. "Who?" He asked.

"The robber." The boy answered disparagingly, "He already succeeded."

"Succeeded?" He got interested. _Maybe if I keep him occupied…_

"Didn't he?" The boy looked at him coldly. "He already got the money. He
could have kept it and fled to somewhere, but this good-for-nothing gave
up! He is a coward!"

_Coward…_ He sensed the boy's emphasis on that word.

"And you wouldn't be scared in that situation?" He asked.

"Why would I? Do you know how easy it is to flee in this place? Nobody
would be able to touch me."

He shrugged, trying to act casually. "Maybe he changed his mind."

"Huh?"

"Maybe in the last minute, something stopped him from making a big mistake."

The boy frowned. "You mean, like conscience?!" he said, as if his mouth was
filled with gall.

"Something like that."

"Only good-for-nothing says something like that."

"You can't say that." Riona calmly interjected. "Young man, you will
repeat that mistake if you don't think carefully."

Her sudden and forthright remark completely caught them off guard. The boy
stunned and winced. "What is that supposed to mean?"

"Riona, let me handle this." He whispered Riona softly, trying to keep her
away, but Riona remained still. She looked at the kid directly.

"Child, I have been looking after this place for too long, and things like
this won't escape me. You've been measuring for a while, plucking up your
courage. Is this your first time?"

The boy gritted, and he abruptly jumped out of his chair and pulled
something out of his pocket. It was a dirk.

"So what! I am going to rob all your money. Don't try to stop me if you
are smart." The boy started walking toward Riona.

He was anxious now. He carefully searched around, trying to find something
he could use. He threw away the only weapon he had before coming back to
inn. His bag got nothing but clothes.

Riona stood passively. "You still have time. You are still not a criminal
yet."

The boy stood in front of Riona. "Lady, have you ever been a beggar?"

"No…"

He smirked. "Well I have begged, I have lied, and I have stolen. Whatever
that fills my stomach. This is just a natural next step in my path."

"And you are selling your dignity for few coins."

For a while, the boy stared at Riona gamely and unblinkingly, then he
started reaching for the counter. When the boy almost finished grabbing
everything into his bag, he heard footsteps coming from outside. A Mileth
officer wearing green uniform was coming.

"Guard coming!" he blurted out.

The boy turned his head and saw someone coming in. He completely freaked
out, wide-eyed and agape. Suddenly they heard Riona speak.

"Put the dirk down."

The boy stayed frozen. He seemed to have become dumbfounded.

"Put your dirk into your pocket now!" she said gruffly and firmly, "And sit
down."

At that point he realized what Riona was trying to do. "Riona…"

She ignored him. She placed a cup of drink in front of the boy and gave him
an eye gesture. When the guard came in, he merely saw a scene of Mileth
daily life. He was idling on his chair. Riona was looking at the mirror as
usual. The boy was sitting and staring at his coffee.

"Greetings, Riona." The guard only took a cursory look at him and the boy.

He had no choice but to play along. "Hail officer. Patrolling?"

"Pursuing, you may say." Said the guard, tired. "We are chasing an
unidentified bank robber."

"No luck?"

"Not yet… but we suspect he or she is still in town."

_So it's a routine visit._ Riona eye gestured the unnerved kid again to
stay quiet.

"But Mileth is big, and then there's the crypt…"

The guard sighed. "As long as he doesn't leave town, we'll find him." He
took another incautious look at the boy, then left the inn and closed the
door behind him.

After the guard left, nobody in the inn seemed to move for a while. The
boy , his shoulder slightly sagged, kept looking silently at the cup.
Suddenly he stood up, eyes still looking down, put the loot back on the
counter. They heard him murmuring something they did not quite catch.
_Apology!?_ He wasn't sure anymore. Then the kid sprang up and rushed out of the door.
It's over.

After hearing the door bump, his mind finally relaxed. No one was harmed.
Riona took a brief inventory of the items, and smiled warmly at him.

"He didn't take anything. I believe this is the kid's first time. I wish I
did the right thing."

He nodded in agreement. When the kid realized that he almost fell into
robbery today, it could well be a deterrent for the rest of his life. He
hoped so.

He stepped out and walked to the backside of the inn. He heaved a sigh of
release, draining out all his inside tension along with it, grateful for the
relief that Mileth's autumn air provided. At a distance, he saw a dim
swirling light from the direction of town gate. _So the mass must be over
now…_ The light was hazy in the distance, but as he stared into it he felt
it's still beautiful, almost halcyon. He started to understand why Riona
chose to remain behind that counter for so many years.

He searched though his bag and took out a cloth. It was a black mask. He
stared at the mask briefly, and folded it into a dense mass. _This is the
last time anyone will see this._ Then with all his might, he tossed the
cloth far into the middle of the riverbed, and waited for the stream to
carry it away.

Only, it didn't flow away, just slowly sunk into deep Mileth River.

THE END
