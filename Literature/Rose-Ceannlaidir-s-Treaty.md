# Ceannlaidir's Treaty

_by Rose in **Dark Ages**_

It had been a long night of fervent prayer. I knelt by the temple statue with a blade in my hand, head bent, and the faint impression of its draco-engraved hilt beginning to imprint my forehead. Ceannlaidir would speak to me. I knew it. But no voice came.

I must have dreamt then, for I saw Pravat and a glorious battle. When I awoke, an unfamiliar knight bent over me, his mien concerned, or perhaps disapproving. I hastened to straighten my mystic gown and receive him.

“I am Sir Tybalt. I am sent from the Court of Loures with an urgent message. Where is your superior?” he demanded. Taking his mailed hand, I gratefully arose and replied with as much dignity as I could feign under the circumstances, “The high priests have journeyed abroad to spread the word of Ceannlaidir. I am…not of high rank,” I murmured. Sir Tybalt sighed and paced. “Milady, circumstances are dire. This message must be delivered to the Goblin King with haste. King Bruce does not wish our direct involvement to be known. My squire can accompany you in disguise to show you the way, but it is the King’s desire the message be delivered by your temple. We wish to avoid…certain complications of Court involvement.”

I considered briefly before replying. Ceannlaidir had spoken long ago to one of our number and made it clear we should ally with the goblin peoples, for they are stronger, and destined to defeat the Grimlok. “I will deliver the message, but surely…a warrior would be a better choice?”

Without replying, he handed me the message, and brought forth a young lad clad in peasant garb. “This is Gorleun, my squire. He knows the path and will represent Loures in an unofficial capacity. All I ask of you is that the message be delivered before the moon renews.” Gorleun bowed deeply and kissed my hand in the ancient gesture of respect a knight owes a lady. I appraised him. He looked nervous, a bit untried, and moreso when Sir Tybalt left to bring word to his King of my acceptance.

The journey to Pravat from Piet is an easy one, but secrecy was of the utmost importance. On the way, I prayed to Ceannlaidir for our success. I had no doubt Grimlok raiding parties were likely to stumble upon us before we ever met with the Goblin King. The Goblin King was a further uncertainty. Despite our allegiance, I was not well-versed in their ways. As parley gifts, I had brought three items blessed by Ceannlaidir and a handful of gems from the temple war-chest. Would it be enough to gain us audience?

Gorleun took the first watch, a situation I was later to regret. I lay down to rest uneasily, my father’s sword clasped tightly in both hands. The night was strangely silent, too silent. Before dawn we would be able to enter Pravat and seek the Goblin encampment. But nothing in war is ever simple. Gorleun, young lad that he was, was asleep when I awoke. With anger, I shook him. “Have you no sense, sir? We are near the front line! Our throats might have been slit as we dreamt!” Gorleun looked abashed and mumbled a frightened apology. At that moment, we heard the high-pitched calls of Grimlok scouts approaching. “Quickly!” I hissed. “We must find the caves and lose ourselves there.” It was known to me that unallied travelers through Pravat are often attacked by both sides. It was imperative that we hide until we could find a goblin guide to lead us to the King’s encampment where we might offer our allegiance and the missive with the royal seal of Loures I had sewn into my skirts. In truth, I would have preferred a battle to glorify Ceannlaidir, but the message could fall into the hands of the Grimlok should we fail. I could smell battle, but the time to bloody Father’s sword would have to wait.

Ceannlaidir must have asked Fiosachd to aid our passage, for we managed to find the caves without being captured. However, as soon as we entered, we walked straight into one of the bloodiest conflicts I have ever seen. Heads and limbs of goblins and grimloks rolled underfoot. Here was a full-scale battle worthy of the tapestries in Loures castle.

Suddenly a goblin spied our intrusion. “… goblin brother?” he grunted suspiciously to Gorleun, completely ignoring the presence of a mere woman. Meanwhile I was seized by a battle-mad Grimlok lass. “Do Grimlok like you?” she demanded, twisting my bare arm hard. Wrenching my arm free, I raised my father’s sword and struck true. The surprised creature gasped once and toppled lifeless, her green hair splayed in a pool of blood. Gorleun seemed to have frozen. There was no time to waste. Whirling on the goblin, I pointed my sword at his chest. “I am goblin sister!” I screamed hoarsely over the battle cries surrounding us. “I see King, NOW.” It was obvious Gorleun’s hesitation in battle had not impressed him. I could see his eyes narrow as he considered whether to accept an order from a woman and an aisling woman at that. On the other hand, I had the advantage of a better weapon. He grunted and spat. I drew a thin line of blood down the front of his chest in the kinship ritual I had only read about. His eyes widened and he grunted again as I offered my arm. Drawing his dagger, he slashed a rather deep gash on my arm and on Gorleun’s heaving chest. “Goblin sister,” he grunted and spat three times. “King near. Come.” This time he ignored Gorleun. And they say goblins aren’t bright.

The Goblin King’s camp was not far from the caves. I noted with some satisfaction it was strategically located, outside the front line, but close enough to send forth warriors in waves. We were escorted in by a contingent of six still-wary goblins. The King and his mage made an impression of strength and determination. As we waited, goblin scouts ran to and fro grunting in their own language. Maps were exchanged. Pots laden with food boiled with goblin rations. I was not surprised the King knew of our earlier battle.

“Eat with us,” he shouted, gesturing at a strip of trampled grass where his guards were enjoying rough bread and stew. I knelt with Gorleun in the muddied grass and accepted a wooden platter. I bit into a chunk of tough bread and after examining the stew, whispered to Gorleun, “Eat only the bread. If you share your stew with another soldier they may not take offense.” I handed my plate of stew to a guard across the fire as Gorleun’s eyes widened. “It would be unfair of us to eat your kills,” I explained appeasingly. Fortunately, the guard was hungry. “Are you ssssaying….they are….c…c…cannibals?” whispered Gorleun urgently. “Of course not,” I snapped. “The meat is Grimlok and aisling.” My comrade looked a bit ill. I wondered how much he had eaten.

When the feast was over and the drumming began, we were at last able to have audience with the Goblin King, who was far in his cups by then. “Why you come? We not need weak aisling help. Aisling make promise, not keep,” he asserted with some bitterness. “I bring letter for you, Great King.” I ripped it from the hem of my skirt. “Other King sends. Aisling King sends to you. Great God Ceannlaidir sends Great King these gifts from my temple,” I added, handing him a shield and gauntlets blessed by Ceannlaidir. “These will strengthen your resolve,” I declared, knowing it to be truth. He examined them carefully, and raised them to his nose, perhaps smelling their magic. “Is good gift. You bring more?” I handed him the small pouch of gems. He grunted again, almost satisfied. I was beginning to wonder why he had not read the letter yet. “and this….” I added, throwing down the head of the Grimlok I had killed. The entire camp paused. “You have killed enemy of the Goblins?” He relaxed slightly. “We not trust aislings often, but you prove. This Bruce King, something he want from goblins. I know this. Aisling King always want help. You bring one more thing, then I make you true goblins.”

The mage stepped forward and explained to us about dark rocks, stones of great magical power. He hinted they could be used to call what he called “Great Power from Elder Day.” What he did not say was why the goblins could not harvest these stones for themselves, but I knew. Only aislings who have studied the ancient tome of Conix Lore can harvest the substance without ruining its properties. Here was our final test, and a dangerous one it was. The stones are found in the heart of the battleground.

Gorleun and I armed ourselves and went with the next goblin raiding party. Fortune was on our side. Gorleun had also read the ancient tome. Between us, we would be able to gather a significant number of stones, enough to impress the Goblin King. We shed much blood that hour, fighting our way to the ancient Conix stones. Digging in battle is not easy, but I managed to gather half a dozen before the battle became too thick. My companion managed to find three but his mind was numbed by their draining nature after this. Still, nine stones would contribute to the goblin effort immensely.

We returned to the King, bearing the heavy rocks. Again, goblin celebrations were in full sway and we were offered food and drink, but eventually I was able to get to the King’s side. “I bring you the dark rocks. Six many. May Ceannlaidir make your army stronger. My companion brings you…” and then I realized Gorleun was gone. “brings you some rocks later I think.” I hastened to cover. The king snatched the Conix with glee and added another gash to my swollen arm. “You goblin sister now. So I say.” He reached for the letter I had delivered before and shoved it into my hand. “What it say?” he demanded. I cursed myself for a fool. Why had we believed he could read it? I felt odd breaking the seal of King Bruce, and hoped Tybalt would understand. Gorleun had apparently fled the stew and the King didn’t look in the mood to wait. I read it to him, all three pages of praise and promise, and one or two wee favors King Bruce wished done in exchange. The Goblin King was no fool. He had seen it coming. It was a deal he would accept, for now. I dare not reveal all the secrets of that letter.

My first mission to Ceannlaidir was a success, but for one further incident. Later as I drank goblin mead with the mage, a cohort of goblins arrived bearing an aisling body. “This one traitor. He take stones to Grimlok queen!” announced the leader. I casually put my hand to my blade, sensing I might have one more battle coming, but the Goblin King was in a good mood. “You go now. You tell Bruce King goblins not cheat.” I was more than ready to leave and curtseyed to him as I hoisted my pack. As for Gorleun? All I can say is, if you visit the goblins, don’t eat the stew.
