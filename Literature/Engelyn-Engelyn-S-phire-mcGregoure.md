# Engelyn S'phire mcGregoure

_by Engelyn in **Dark Ages**_

It is dark. I can hear the incessant 'drip, drip' of some far off leak, a place where the moisture from decaying bodies seeped through walls of dirt and stone, falling, sending shivers down my spine to my very soul. I hear the scratch of little toes, tiny claws and the sometimes-faint squeak of vicious rodents. As I walk I unknowingly stumbled into the webs of venomous spiders, angering those arachnid mothers, who chased me screaming in indignation at my offense. They’re sticky spawn pods cling to my hair, entangling it. These though, are the least of the maddeningly sick creatures that traverse these depths. It is a folly to feel alone in this place. You can turn and catch out of the corner of your eye, the glowing yellow gaze of terrifying monsters. I'm not ashamed to say I am afraid. But, I have been afraid like this before… Though now I don't know if it was a blessing or a curse: this fear I once experienced…

## I

William Luke Dakotah had always been my friend. Since we were kids we’d been around each other, and we told each other everything…

When I was young I lived a simple life. I was the daughter of a farm hand. I lived just outside of Mileth. I played in the Eastern Woodlands, where I became accustomed to the creatures there. I was never afraid of anything. My childhood friends were all boys. We dared each other to touch the praying mantises and tease the bees. I couldn't stand the doll playing and dressmaking activities my sisters so loved. My mother was a dressmaker, she adored my sisters, but I was always Daddy's little girl. I started helping him in Master Ewan's fields when I was only 6. Hitching the oxen and planting seeds of wheat and barley. Master Ewan sold the wheat to Oona, the Mileth baker, so she could make her bread. He sold the barley to Aingeal, the Mileth tavern keeper, where it was manufactured into ale, wine and rum. Many locals worked in Master Ewan's fields including the fathers of many of my childhood friends. So you see how we had things in common, William and I. William lived next to me, and our fathers were good friends. We grew up together…

When I was 7 he protected me from Rudy D’Anel, the local bully. I was taking Master Ewan’s finest mare to the animal healer. The horse had come down with colic in the night. I’d found her that morning lying in the pasture; I put my ear to her belly and heard no sound from her stomach. So I ran and told my father and he sent me with her to the healer. Rudy liked to pull girl’s hair. My long, messy, blonde locks were a prime target. As I walked the mare down the path to town, William followed me. With the grace of a warrior he danced upon the low stone wall along the path, keeping pace with me and my charge. When he spotted a snake sunning itself by the pond he wandered off to bother it. Rudy, who’d been eyeing us as we passed his cottage hurried out to block my path. With his bare feet covered in dust and his clothes not much better off, he still had the nerve to cross his arms as if he were a Loures guard waiting to collect a fee for my passing. His blue eyes glared at me as he stuck his chin up in the air. I could see right up his red, freckled, turned-up nose. I didn’t see anything in there… I always knew he didn’t have a brain. Regardless, he was still a good foot and a half taller then me. So when he grabbed my hair and gave a mighty tug, I yelped in pain, releasing the hold I had on the mare’s reins. William looked up at my cry, seeing my distress he ran over and socked Rudy one good, right in the eye. Rudy never messed with me again.

At 8 I tried to stay out all night in the Woodland. I made a camp with a tent and fire and all. William came with me. When our parents found us we were wrapped in one each other’s arms. The fire had been swept away by a chill pre-winter breeze and we were both shaking with cold. He was 11.

When I was 10 I hated him, as girl and boy children often hate one another. He was 13.

By 12 we were fast friends again.

I I

When I turned 16 I attended my first Harvest Ball. I had to dress like a girl,… and act like a girl,… and brush my hair like a girl. It was disgusting. My mother said I looked like a dream, I didn’t believe her because I knew inside I wasn’t meant to be dressed up and poised. I knew I should be mean and tough. I knew I was ugly. Who was I to show my face among the public? I felt like a clown the way my Mother pinched my checks to flush them, just before we entered the square. The way the other girls looked at me affirmed what a mistake I was making by showing up. It was all my mother’s fault. I gave a steamy look for good measure. She absently smiled at me as she left my side to go say hello to her friends from the Tailor Brody’s shop.

I sighed and mumbled under my breath, taking time to scratch the inside of my elbow where some poisonous plant had left a bright red rash.

I looked up and spied a seat, unfortunately, it was across the makeshift dance floor. Quickly I maneuvered across the expanse, trying my best to pretend the passing dancers were swaying branches of some enchanted tree… I was very uncomfortable.

I sat a tore at the hem of my gown, it was a dark blue motley of silk. I concentrated of picking the fibers apart and thought about how silly this was. The purpose of the Harvest Ball was for young townspeople to meet. It was hoped that from these meetings courtships would develop and eventually marriages would bloom. Who thought of marriage at 16?

I jumped, startled as something bumped hard into the back of my head. I turned directly into the powdered sneer of Elainea Havens, who could have been my archenemy. Close behind her stood Heidi and Jensenk, her friends, if one such as her could have friends.

“Oh, I’m sorry. I didn’t mean to…Well you know,” she apologized, the sneer never leaving her rose petal painted mouth.

She turned to her two companions. “Doesn’t Lyn look just ravishing t’night Heidi?” she chuckled, still grinning evilly. “Jen we really must see her mother about gowns for the fair. I’m sure she could scrounge up some old curtains to tailor ours out of too. Of course, It would still look like the latest Loures fashion.” Her sinister cackle droned on and on in my head. The two girls that she addressed, her lackeys that followed her everywhere, encouraged her with grins and laughs of their own.

I didn’t bother to answer her blatant challenge. I knew better. It was really just a matter of time before they got bored and would just leave. There wasn’t a sight I liked more than the glistening of Elainea’s chestnut hair as it slowly receded into the distance, swaying along with the hips so many of the boys admired. How they could stand to think of her as something desirable to obtain I’ll never know.

Unfortunately, now she loomed over me, like some viscous gargoyle escaped from Dubhaidm castle. I looked up at her from under my eyelashes, I’m sure my green eyes flashed with indignation. “Is there some reason ye came over here?” My voice was set with steely resolve to be polite and not make a scene. Every word was forced through a clenched jaw.

Elainea’s sparkling crème colored gown rustled as she settled into the seat beside me. She leaned close to whisper in my ear; “I came only to tell you that your days of childhood friendship with William are over. He has grown up and now he will leave you…for me. I get what I want; what I want is the best; he is the best, so, he is mine. There is nothing you can do about it.”

I chewed my lip, staring straight ahead as she spoke, here nearness gave me the creeps, and it was hard to keep still.

“What no smart retort? I thought you’d put up some fight… After all, I’m sure you’ve fantasized about a life with William forever.”

I stood and turned to look at her, “I’m sorry to say but ye are sorely mistaken. William and I have never been more than friends. And, If I desired us to be more, we would be.” I walked away, trying my best to look over-confident, but I am sure my steps faltered.

Fuming I found a seat far away from Elainea, to think she thought she could get under my skin. I felt nothing for William like she described. What he and I had was far deeper than teenage lust. I was sure.

I was surprised when I felt a heavy hand on my shoulder. I looked up into those brilliant green eyes and strong, set jaw, and my anger melted.

“You look upset, may I sit?” said William.

I smiled, “Why not?”

He drew back a chair and sat, his step still had the grace I remembered him having when he was boy.

“So what is it that is bothering you? Oh wait! I bet I know. It’s the dress isn’t it? Well let me reassure you that you look positively dreadful in it. The ugliest girl I’ve ever seen. What possessed you to come out in public?” his jovial smile radiated like the sun.

“Oh thank ye so much. I told my mother I though the color was wrong. Red looks much better on me, don’t you think? She said Raenyse needed the red fabric for hers and I would just have to settle. Woe is me! I will never find a husband to take care of me and fill me with babies,” my grin was slightly forced, he didn’t seem to notice.

“I’d feel sorry for your children, cursed with the infamous McGregoure homeliness,” he dared to start laughing.

I tried to smack him but he caught my wrist, still laughing.

“How extremely rude!” I started to stand.

“Aww, don’t run away…I didn’t mean it honest,” still he laughed. “I’ll make it up to you. Here, I’ll go get you something to eat.” He stood and pushed me back into my seat. “Back in a flash. Promise.”

“Well… I guess,” I said, but he was already on his way. My gaze followed him across the dance floor, admiring that ever-present grace. I was so caught up in watching him I didn’t see the eminent danger approaching, until she was right up on him.

I I I

As I walked towards the dais where the food was set up on buffet, I thought about what my parents had said. I kept hearing my mother’s voice, ‘Won’t you consider her, Dear? Marrying Elainea will mean a life of nobility. Her parents are both very powerful Aislings and, although the spark of New Sight was not passed on you her, she is a charmingly beautiful woman. All the other boys think so. Your 19 and almost 20, you need to start considering making a life for yourself! We don’t want you working in the fields for the rest of your life. Your life is wasting away and all you can do is spend time with that McGregoure girl! If she is even a girl. She wears boys clothes most of the time, and she is usually just as dirty as any of your baby brothers. Don’t you long for a girl with grace and elegance? Won’t you please consider our words…’

It made me angry that they presumed to make my decisions for me. Then again they did have a valid point. Recently I’d felt something calling me. Perhaps it was time for me to marry. Yet that didn’t seem to be what was beckoning me. The thing that called seemed dark, perhaps even dangerous. Nothing like marrying a girl I hardly knew and starting a family. I just wish I knew what was happening to me lately. The only person that wasn’t asking something of me was Engelyn. I just wish everyone else would leave me alone so I could sort it out.

I was so lost in thought that I must have bumped into her, because suddenly Elainea was right in front of me. More than right in front of me she was pressed up against me, arms wrapped around my waist, I thought, to keep from falling.

“Oh you big oaf!” she exclaimed.

“Ah! I’m sorry Elai-“

“Oh, William it’s you!” she cut in, a smile playing on her lips, “don’t worry about it. I’m not hurt. You did startle me though, but it’s all okay now…” Her eyes followed her hand as she ran it down my chest, straightening my already straight tunic.

I stepped away from her accidental embrace, uncomfortable. “Well, if your okay then I think I’ll continue on with my mission to get something to eat.”

“Oh no don’t go! I was looking for you.”

“Uh, you were?”

“Yes, I was. Don’t be so surprised,” her smile was inferring more than her words.

“Oh, well, Okay,” I smiled back, unsure of how to respond, she was so beautiful. She looked so expectant…

“Well, you look beautiful tonight. That dress must have cost a small fortune,” I cringed, hoping I’d said the right thing.

“Oh, It’s nothing,” she smiled, putting a hand to her bare shoulder, which drew my attention to the sparkling jewels at her neck. “My Daddy paid for it.”

My eyes wandered from her neck to the neckline of her dress, which spilt just a hint of cleavage.

“Ahem,” her voice brought me back to the conversation like a thunderbolt.

I stammered, “Well…What was it you…you wanted?”

“Well,” she smiled playfully and looked away, “I was wondering if you’d like to dance with me.” She took my hands and pressed her body against mine, like it had been when we bumped into one another. She stared into my eyes, her sultry lips were inches from mine, “So, would you?”

I couldn’t refuse if I wanted to.

I I I I

I watched as Elainea and William danced. I felt like I was going to throw up. I couldn’t stand to see her clinging fingers clutching his shirt. The worst part was, I didn’t know why it effected me so. Surely I didn’t want William to be with Elainea, just because I knew she was evil inside, but still, I shouldn’t feel the way I do, almost jealous, in a way.

I saw her lean close and whisper something softly, in his ear. She looked right at me as she said it, and smiled. I felt like I was going to gag. Literally, bile rose in my throat.

No one noticed when I left the party. I just couldn’t watch them anymore. So I went and sat at a bench away from the festivities, though I could still hear the music playing.

As I sat I wondered about the future. Where was my life heading?

I I I I I

I saw Engelyn get up and leave, though I couldn’t fathom why. I thought maybe that she had just gotten sick of all the people. She never was very social. It sort of made her mysterious though. I think most people thought of her that way, mysterious. I knew better though. I knew her inside and out. She was more sensitive than she let people know. I knew I’d better go after her, something could be really wrong. But… It felt good dancing with Elainea. She was so beautiful, chestnut hair, wide hips, startling violet eyes. Who would want to leave?

“Is something wrong Will?” Elainea asked, concern pouting on her lips and in the frown of her brow.

“No, nothing,” we turned in time with the music. Her body felt heavenly against mine.

“Good,” she smiled, “doesn’t this feel right to you?” She put her head on my shoulder. Her hair was so soft and her hot breath on my neck sent shivers of anticipation of the greater things promised in that question.

“As anything could be…” I let that hang. Guilt was nagging at me as we danced. I knew Engelyn needed me. The song was almost through, I decided I would find her after. Who knew my parents would be right about Elainea? There was no denying her steamy beauty.

We twirled and swirled around and around. I caught glances of my mother smiling from her table, sitting next to my father and Master Ewan and his wife.

The music was bouncy and joyous. It seemed contagious. Everyone was smiling and laughing. Dozens of couples spun and interweaved in a high-spirited stupor. Finally the song ended. I let go of Elainea’s hand and waist, and took a step back.

She asked with a sly smile, “where do you think your going?”

I frowned, “I have to go see what’s wrong with Engelyn. She just got up and left. Something has upset her.”

Then smile finally left her face, “but I thought…Ugh… Go then.” With that I was dismissed it seemed.

“Well, thanks for the dance anyway. Maybe I can coax her back and we could turn another?” I asked hopefully.

“Sure, sure,” she seemed distracted and upset.

I frown and shrugged, “okay then. See you,” and walked away.

I I I I I I

I jumped as someone sat down beside me. I knew it was him before he even opened his mouth. I refused to look at him. If I had I’d have forgiven him straight away. I wanted to be mad.

“So Kid, what’s going on?”

I though for a moment, “nothing ye need to be concerned with.”

“Aww don’t come off that way. We’ve been friends for long enough. What is it?”

“Really it’s nothing. I guess I’m just not ready for all this, being a woman and all.” I looked up into his eyes, I’m sure I looked like a pathetic liar. Though, when I said those words I couldn’t help but feel a tinge of truth in them

“Aww, well sure your not, “ his eyes smiled, “…your just a little dirty kid,” his grin widened.

“Hmph,” I glared. “I guess I’ll always be,” and I added in a voice barely a whisper, “…at least, in ye’r eyes.”

“What’s that Lyn? What did you say? Your not serious are you? Of course it’s hard for me to think of you as a young woman. We’ve been together forever. I’ve seen you naked. Women are mysterious. There is not mystery about you, none at all. I know them all. I know all your secrets,” he grinned and pushed me back lightly. “After all you are only 16.”

“You barely act that even,” he went on, “always off on some or another imaginary adventure.

“Stop, just stop,” I interrupted his mini-tirade. “Why don’t ye just go back inside and finish whatever ye started with Elainea, I’m sure she misses you already,” my sour expression betrayed me.

“Is that what this is about? Elainea? I knew you hated the girl but this? She really isn’t all that bad. In fact I like her more and more now that I’ve started spending time with her.”

I wished right then that he would fade away. I started to turn and walk towards home, even though the walk would seem like deochs on foot.

“Where are you going? Stop, would you,” he grabbed my arm and pulled me back.

I was so upset and startled by his physical aggression that I balled my fist and swung at him. My punch glanced off his chin.

“Jeez, what’s wrong with you? What was that for?” he rubbed his chin and glared at me.

“Uh…I’m sorry. You frightened me,” I lied.

“Yeah sure, they wasn’t an I’m-scared punch, it was a leave-me-alone-you-jerk punch. My chin knows the difference,” he grimaced.

He pressed, “What’s going on with you, your really acting strange.”

My body shook with rage and compassion, and a million other emotions. Tears started to stream down my cheeks. I’d only cried in front of William once ever. That was when I fell of my horse and broke my leg. Even then it wasn’t the racking sobs I felt crouching in my throat, waiting to pounce out at him. “I don’t know what’s wrong with me. I just don’t know.” I could tell William was taken aback.

“Sweet Danaan, something is terribly wrong,” he finally realized, and swept me up in his arms. “I am so sorry.”

I went limp in his embrace. I didn’t know what to feel. I fought the urge to push him away. Loving the way it felt and hating the way it felt, I was so confused. We stood like that for what seemed like hours. My arms felt so heavy, like they were weighed with Hy-Brasyl. Finally I overcame the crushing weight and lifted them to wrap around his waist. I cried on his shoulder that evening as I confessed all that I was feeling.

I I I I I I I

As that year past a secret affair developed between William and I. He kept it secret from his parents because they thought he was courting Elainea. Elainea thought so too, and she rubbed it in my face every chance she got. I was so hard to hold my tongue. I was only a child then, and I knew not what I was getting myself into. Needless to say I was crushed when I got the news.

“What do ye mean ye’r going away?” I screeched.

“Baby, I have to go,” William replied.

We were in a far field on Master Ewan’s property, hiding in a shack that stored scythes for harvesting. His hand was on my waist as we stood near the open entrance.

“No ye don’t,” I stepped away from his embrace. “Ye don’t.”

“But I do. There are things more than you can understand calling me: voices, and longings. They promise me all I’ve ever wanted, all I’ve ever dreamed. I must go.”

I saw the spark in his eye glint in the darkness, and I hated it. It made me want to rip his eyes out to rid him of it. When I turned 17 and he turned 20 the voices that had been calling William since he was a child finally won him over. He’d become an Aisling. It seemed so predictable, all the time that we spent together as children I’d known he’d leave our small township, and become a brave Aisling warrior. I knew it all along, yet, there was no way I could accept it now. I thought I was falling in love with him. The tears that had not been shy since that night at the Harvest Ball again began to fall. I couldn’t stop them. I felt empty.

“You understand don’t you,” he put his hands on my shoulders, his big hands. “You have to understand.”

“I… I understand,” I said slowly. The words were forced but the relieved expression on his face meant the world to me, even though it broke my heart into pieces.

“Dear Gods, thank you, I knew you would.”

My heart turned to icicles, which then pierced my skin, prickling it with goose bumps. “So, what happens now?”

He let go of my shoulders and drew himself up. “I have to go. I leave for Loures in the morning, and there is still so much to do.”

I looked down. “So I guess this is it. I guess this is goodbye.”

“Aye, I guess this is goodbye,” he watched me I knew, though I did not look. He opened his mouth to say something, then closed it quickly and turned. He didn’t look back as he strode into the darkness. After a few minutes I started to chase after him. I stumbled and fell in the night. I did not have the heart to regain my feet. The world crashed around me.

I I I I I I I I

For years my life was routine. I worked the fields, and for all anyone was concerned, I would never be a real woman. I would never marry, never bear children. I was just another boy, dirty from the fields. My parents soon grew so disgusted with me that they threw me out. Even my Dad, who’d been my best friend all my life, aside from William, had no pity. I was a daughter gone wrong. I felt like I could never love again.

“I brought your apples Oona.”

“Thanks,” she replied frankly. No one ever felt like talking to me. I was a town disgrace. Since my parents had ousted me I’d made a shelter for myself in the Eastern Woodlands. For money I collected things I found there and sold them. Apples, honey, wolf skins, mantis eyes, pretty much anything I could get my hands on. No, I never killed the beasts myself, but enough young Aislings came along, killed and left their loot. So I thought myself quite the civil servant by cleaning up the place.

I found a seat on a hard wooden bench away from most of the people in the bakery. Sullenly I ate the barley soup I’d ordered. I was 20 and my life had no direction. I would waste away into nothing, and no one would ever remember me.

I stirred the bowl of cold, tasteless soup and watched the people talking and laughing. I was jealous I knew, of their happiness. Mine had been stolen away by the Aisling gift. Since William had left I felt so alone and empty. I saw other Aislings at the bar, talking of the grand lives they led and the great battles they’d fought. I saw a warrior who would be about William’s age talking to a young girl who wore the dress of a rogue. Her long ringlets of red hair glistened and blazed like fire in the weak torch light. They seemed very interested in one another. The girl couldn’t have been more than 15, yet her eyes shone with a gift I couldn’t understand nor comprehend. The warrior’s eyes reflected that same gift, and so they connected, despite their age difference. I hated them both for it.

So engrossed was I in my own self-pity, I didn’t notice when a brown-haired fellow came along and sat right down opposite me at my table. In fact, I didn’t know how long he’d been sitting there. It seemed like he’d appeared out of no where. I jumped up and my knees banged painfully against the underside of the tabletop.

“Oh, careful there Miss,” his smile was silly.

“What to ye want?” I questioned immediately, thinking he was some kind of authority figure, looking to chase me away from the only home I had. They were always bothering me.

“Only the company of the fair maid like ye,” the stranger replied, his smile resolved to stay on his face, despite my rude reaction.

I must have looked extremely doubtful because he went on, “…but I know one with beauty such as ye’rs would never settle for the company of a trickster such as I.”

“Beauty?” I motioned vaguely to my matted blond hair and smudged face. “Surely ye jest. In fact I am sure ye jest. It is not one bit funny. Please, leave me be.”

“Ahh, but I won’t. I can see past all that dirt. I know who ye are inside.” He leaned close and his eyes twirled. It made me dizzy to look into them. “I can show ye who ye are…”

“What do ye mean…?”

“Ah. But I can only show ye, not explain. Do ye wish to see it?” His words were quick and hard to follow. I’d grown unaccustomed to long conversation.

“I guess…”

“Lovely, come along then,” and with that he grabbed my elbow and towed me along. Soon we arrived in Tailor Brody’s shop.

I looked around, “I doubt and wardrobe change will show me the real me inside,” I said sarcastically.

The stranger only grinned as he approached Brody. They spoke silent words I could not here. Suddenly the strangest feeling washed over me. I felt my face and hair changing. The tangles that had grown into knots because of years of abandonment twisted and untangled like snakes retracting to strike. I a feeling of peace washed over me and I fainted.

I I I I I I I I I

I watched the girl as she slept. She was incomprehensibly beautiful. Long blonde hair, and from what I’d seen before she went under, striking green eyes, and that perfect figure. It was a shame she hid it all under dirt and ragged clothing. I wondered what her story was. I think I was about to find out. Her eyes fluttered open.

“What? What’s going on?”

“Do not be afraid,” I replied.

“Who are ye?”

“My name is Ranux, I’m a rogue. I used my hair styling skill to transform ye’r hair into what it used to look like. The change must have effected ye more so because it removed all the dirt from ye’r face and body. Then ye fainted. Must have been my striking good looks, eh?” I grinned.

“What!?,” She sat up quickly and scrambled to get away from me.

“Relax, ye’r going to be fine. I made a promise, now let me fulfill it.”

“What are ye talking about? I don’t even know you!”

“Remember I was going to show ye the real you. Well she’s escaped, there’s nothing ye can do about it now.” I grabbed a hand mirror and handed it to her. “Here, take a look.”

She looked uncertain but finally she dared a peek. Her expression was unreadable until her jaw dropped.

“It has been 3 years since I last looked at myself. My, I don’t know what to say. I’m a woman…”

“Indeed ye are my Dearest.”

“Oh what am I?” her distress was poignant as she threw the mirror down. I saw tears bubbling up in her eyes.

“What is it?” I leaned close and reached out a hand of comfort, but I was afraid to touch her, thinking she’d react strangely.

“I don’t know who I am! I have been lost for ages! Please, please, tell me who I am,” her sobs were racking. I could not see the cause of her distress. I knew only that her soul needed comfort and something to cling to. I braved the storm and laid a hand on the Beauty. She did not react at all to my touch, and her tears still spilt in droves.

“…Tell me who I am. Please. Please,” her eyes shone with a sickening madness. I feared I’d driven her over the edge.

I took her up in my arms and whispered in her ear, “Ye are Beauty, my Lovely. Ye are Beauty.”

And she cried into my neck, cried until she was so exhausted she slept. I feared to move. I did not want to wake her. I soon too slept. She was in my arms when I awoke.

I I I I I I I I I I

I stayed in Ranux’s room at the inn for three weeks. At first all I did was cry. But I soon discovered it would do me no good. As I started to feel better I realized something about Ranux, something that disturbed me far greater than his being a stranger. Ranux was an Aisling. His eyes didn’t glint with the sharp clarity, like William’s on that last night. Ranux’s were dull, yet the light was still there, submissive, yet, ever present.

I grew to not hate the Gift so much. Its incarnation in Ranux was friendly to me, friendly and loving.

I knew my life was changing, yet it felt so odd. I was experiencing an emotion I’d never felt before. I felt very lonely when Ranux left me at the inn. It was like I constantly longed to have him around. After three weeks he told me he was moving on. He offered to bring me along, if I was willing. I accepted.

We spent months on the road. One mission led to the next. Everywhere I was surrounded my Aislings of a great many classes and powers. They all looked at us strange. It was uncommon for an Aisling like Ranux to travel when a mundane such as I. Mundanes were known for their fear of the world outside their town. I feared my town, what did I have to fear from the outside? Felt safe as long as I was with Ranux.

We spent each night under the stars, wrapped in one another’s arms. He was like a gift from the Gods. He changed my life forever. I knew this time I had fallen into love. It was so much deeper than anything I’d felt before.

“Tomorrow we will reach Mileth, My Dear,” Ranux said, frowning, “I ye sure your ready for this?”

I smiled in reassurance to him, “I have to face my past. Besides, no one is even going to recognize me. I’ve changed so much in these few months.” My hand reached out and clasped his, I squeezed it as we walked. I wore a simple traveling gown. But still, a gown. I’d shed my old self and finally grown anew. I was a woman now. Loved,… really this time, by a man. And a powerful Aisling man at that. I was not ashamed to show my womanhood. Instead I wore it like a proud banner, well, as proud as it could be in mundane clothing.

I I I I I I I I I I I

As we approached the Mileth gates a chill went down my spine. I ignored it. I would be calm and confident. We’d returned for the Harvest Ball where we had plans to announce our forthcoming marriage.

The day led to night without incident. No one really noticed me except as a stranger. I was thankful that I did not run into my parents at all. Surely that would have been a mess. Though I knew they could not be avoided, for they would surely bring my youngest sister, Najella to her first Harvest Ball this night. If I remembered correctly she would be 16 this year.

Ranux bought me the most beautiful gown he could afford. Truly it was more lovely a sight than I had ever seen. It looked like one of the gowns the master Aisling priestesses wore. It sparkled with light and I almost felt ashamed wearing it. I would embarrass all the young girls whose hopes were so high and set on meeting their husbands this night. Ranux insisted I have it.

So I dressed, and we went, never realizing the danger that lurked.

We danced and I knew everyone wondered who we could be, an Aisling man and a strange mundane woman. Everyone stared.

We were a spectacle of ages.

I leaned close and whispered in his ear as we danced to a slower song, “Let’s get away from here. I’ve nothing to prove to these people. My parents have not even shone up yet. I’d much rather dance with ye alone to the music of the woods.” I grinned with the sly smile of a woman in love.

We turned and walked towards the exit of the dance floor, my eyes were trained on the beautiful face of my lover. I did not see him approaching. I don’t know if I’d had recognized him if I did. Scars of Sgrios were tattooed on every limb of his body. Even his face was covered with them. He’d grown so large that he looked like one of the monsters Ranux and I had encountered in the deep pits of the Piet Sewers. I knew terror in that moment.

I I I I I I I I I I I I

While William was away becoming a warrior he was being brainwashed. Sqrios had been calling to him for deochs. William began killing without regret. He never mourned. He’d forgotten Engelyn as soon has his blade drew its first blood. His soul was a blackened, shriveled thing, eaten up by hated and lust. He was possessed by a demon so strong that it could never be extracted from him. Evil had made him powerful and he was too strong for any force to stand against, even Love.

I tried not to look into his flashing eyes, but they bore into me. I could feel them undressing me in an instant. I knew he recognized me. He’d known me too long to forget the way I looked.

Ranux frowned at him, not at all daunted by such an imposing figure, “Excuse me, Sir.” He leaned up next to me and whispered fiercely, “Do ye know this guy?”

“Aye, I once did.” I couldn’t breath, his bloody stare was so intense.

“Yes,” William spoke for the fist time, “she knows me well.” His grin was sinister, the stuff of nightmares. I felt bile and madness rising in my throat. “In fact, I think she’d like to spend some time getting reacquainted, wouldn’t you Lyn?”

“That I doubt,” Ranux shot, his instincts about the man were impeccable.

“Oh, I really think she does. She may not know it yet, but she does,” he spoke slowly, his voice had a ghostly, perverted echo, as if Sgrios himself was speaking.

“I… I-” I didn’t know what to say, so I just ran. I ran because I was frightened I knew. Frightened at what William had become. I was nauseated at the thought that I’d once kissed that man, that horrible, possessed man. I was sick that I’d missed him, that I’d let three years of my life slip away for nothing. I wanted to die.

I heard Ranux following me and after a time I slowed. I allowed him to catch me. As I regained my breath he began to question me, and so I told him all about my past with William. I cried then and cried and cried. Terror was still on my heels though. It’d never left my side.

I I I I I I I I I I I I I

That night I slept in fits and starts until finally I rested comfortably. It seemed I’d slept for bare minute before I was slapped awake by the meaty fist of a huge hand. There was a sheen of sweat on my entire body. No, not sweat, it was thicker. My mind wasn’t functioning. I looked around to find Ranux but I could not find him. I knew we’d ended to in a room at the Mileth inn. It was a mystery to my slogging mind where he could be. My vision was blurry. I didn’t see the first mind-shattering blow coming. I felt it. I crumpled to the floor. Then I realized what the thick sweat stuff was, blood. And I saw the body…

I awoke crying. I’d been dreaming. My sobs immediately woke Ranux too, though he was never quite awake when he first arose. He wrapped his arms around me and muttered, “Everything will be okay…”

The next day we resolved to leave Mileth by sunset. I could sense the presence of the evil that possessed William, and I knew it hunted me. I didn’t know why but it did.

At midnight that day we set up camp, ironically we were a mere mile from where my parents used to live, in a clearing. That clearing had once housed the dreams of two young children, a boy and girl that looked like a boy. In that clearing they had played for hours as children. In that clearing they had grown up together. William and I spent our childhood here.

I slept, comforted by the fact that we had escaped the Evil at least for the night, I couldn’t have been more mistaken.

I I I I I I I I I I I I I I

I stalked them through the night. I knew the place she was taking him. I knew where she was going. I knew without the voice telling me. I’d known forever that it would happen here. He told me when I was a boy and we played here. I’d possess her this night. The voices had driven me insane in the search. For months I’d chased her and her rogue through Temiur. Finally I would have my penance for all my suffering. I could never sit and enjoy myself because I was always pressed, pressed to find the girl. That blonde girl, she threatened the voice, I knew, she threatened Sgrios. He knew what she would become if his seed was not planted in her. He knew. I didn’t know it was her, honest I didn’t, I didn’t know it was Lyn. It was out of my control though. She’d always been a pretty girl. I knew her terrified eyes would haunt me though, haunt me like Sgrios. I would escape a greater danger by following his orders though. I knew I would. I had to do it. I had to do it to end my sentence.

So, I took the boy first, the rogue. I beat him good. He was a bloody mess. I couldn’t tell if he was dead or alive. I left him in the bushes.

Then I moved to her. She felt me coming, and she tried to run.

I I I I I I I I I I I I I I I

Time changes all people,

And I once was changed,

It came all at once,

The night terror raged.

On this night I ran,

Sweat poured down my face,

I tried to escape,

But evil kept up the pace.

On the night I was changed,

My dress was dirty and torn,

On the night I was chased,

I didn't know I was to be re-born.

I stumbled and I fell,

Evil grabbed my wrists,

As I screamed in panic,

I was hit with his fists.

My head swam,

Everything was going black,

I'm glad I couldn't feel it,

When he pulled my dress back.

I awoke in the leaves,

It seemed blasphemy for the sun to shine,

Everything hurt,

After I cried I made a vow,

It was time go hunting,

## Epilogue

On the morning I woke I knew I had changed. I was an Aisling, newly awakened. My first day with the New Sight, I met the depths of my terror. I was bloody everywhere from my beating. My insides hurt the worst. William had possessed me in the only way Sgrios could figure to break me. I came to know all that had been hidden from me. I knew my destiny was to become a great and powerful Aisling. I knew I would seek vengeance on the one that would seek to set my soul afire. As I stood and reflected on the many ways I would killed him, Sgrios took hold and raped the last part of me that was untouched, my mind. The pain and loss had driven me over the edge. He knew I was his. I felt the cold embrace of death itself. As darkness crept in around my soul something inside me cried out in anguish.

I’d forgotten something, something deathly important. I couldn’t remember what it was, just that it was warm. Warmth, the warm embrace, someone warm. It was there, flirting on the outskirts of my mind, teasing me away from the darkness that sought to envelop me. I pressed my hands to the sides of my head and screamed in frustration. The warmth beckoned me. I crept closer. I remembered more. I remembered his face. I remember his smile. I remembered Ranux and though I couldn’t figure out what he meant. He called to me. Sgrios called to me. Sgrios screamed in my head and the scream was so cold. I felt myself sliding back towards oblivion.

Ranux watched me and his eyes were sad. His hands reached for me, he called to me. He wouldn’t leave me alone. I wanted to cry. I wanted to sleep. I hurt everywhere. Everything hurt. He walked towards me. I could he the bloody cuts on his face. He looked so inviting.

Sgrios screamed over and over in my head. I felt like I was going to explode. My eyes were trained on Ranux’s. I still couldn’t understand him. The chill embrace grasped my ankles and my wrists, pulling me back into the underworld. Still Ranux walked towards me, he didn’t seem afraid. Warmth emanated from him. I felt it on my cheek. It healed the bruise there. It spread down my throat and the marks where I’d been bitten faded. I was face to face his him. He did not speak. He leaned and placed his lips to mine, tenderly. Warmth spread through me, and the claws holding my wrists and ankles stopped their incessant pulling. The screaming in my head faded away like an echo.

Ranux gathered my up in his arms. All the blood was gone. He held me gently to him and said, “Ye are Beauty, ye are Beauty.”
