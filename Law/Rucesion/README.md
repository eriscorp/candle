# Rucesion Law

The laws of the Commonwealth of Rucesion, archived by version. Each folder is a snapshot of the code as it stood at that time. The newest folder is the law in force.

The [City and Commonwealth of Rucesion Charter](RL_City-and-Commonwealth-of-Rucesion-Charter.md) sits outside the version folders. It is the founding document of the Commonwealth, enacted in the 7th Deoch in 2000, and has never been revised, so it applies to every version. It is the only text that survives from before 2002.

The 2007 and 2009 folders are partial. They hold the laws that were open for revision at the time, and the rest of the code of that period is lost. The `2009 (Pre)` texts are the oldest surviving copies. The passage dates on them are the last revision before 2009, and the code they belong to is believed to have stood in substantially the same form since 2002. The Commonwealth itself was founded in 2000; apart from the Charter, no text survives from the two regimes of its first two years. The Separation of Powers Act, the Malicious Acts Law and the Trial Procedures Law carry their names from the founding, though no founding text of them survives. The Emergency Empowerment Edict replaced the Martial Law Act in the Deoch 151 revision of 2017.

The `2017 (Pre)` and 2020 folders come from web copies of the code, and each folder takes the date of its copy. The `2017 (Pre)` copy is the "Current Rucesion Law" site. The 2020 copy is the Laws of Rucesion page of the old Library of Loures site. That page omits every passage record, so no 2020 file has one. Five of its laws have the same text as the Deoch 151 code in the 2017 folder, and the Trial Procedures Law has the same text as the 2026 code.

## Structure

Every file follows the same shape:

- `# Title`
- one heading per numbered section: `## 1)`, `### 1.1)`, `#### 1.1.1)`
- a numbered clause that reads as a sentence rather than a title is a paragraph that opens with its bold number, for example `**5.6)** A Judge should define...`
- lettered definitions and enumerations are bullet lists
- the passage record, where the source carries one, is the last line in italics

Text is reproduced as passed. Formatting is the only change. Laws were posted in-game across numbered board posts of about 2,800 characters each; a roman numeral after a law's name in the text ("Integrity Assurance Act IV") is the post index, not a version. Section numbering, spelling and wording are the original, including the duplicated `4.2.1)` in the Codes of Practice and the placement of Protocol Handbook `4.7)` after section 5.

## Citing

Cite by law and section: `IAA 5.3`, `OCC 2.2.4`, `SoP 1.8`. Where a version matters, prefix the folder: `2017 IAA 3.1`.
