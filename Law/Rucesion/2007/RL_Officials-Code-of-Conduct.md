# Official's Code of Conduct

The Commonwealth of Rucesion hereby empowers its’ government to protect and serve the Commonwealth, through the powers and regulations granted through this ordinance. Contravention of this Act by any official will result in action under the Integrity Assurance Act.

## 1) Protocol for all Officials

### 1.1) Duties of Officials

See Separation of Powers Act.

### 1.2) Uniform Regulations

Protocol regarding the wearing of official garb are set out in the Mileth and Rucesion Jurisdiction Accords and in Section 2.2 of this Law.

### 1.3) Protocol for Report Maintenance

Peers are jointly liable for reports to be maintained by holders of their office. Section 3 of the Separation of Powers Act applies to vacant offices. If reports are not maintained, the responsible officials are to be charged with Dereliction of Duty in terms of Section 2.1.2 of the Integrity Assurance Act.

#### 1.3.1) Punishment Reports

Guard Captains are responsible for maintaining on the Justice Cases Board a condensed report of actions taken in the preceding Deoch as well as outstanding warrants.

#### 1.3.2) Exile and Bar Lists

Judges are responsible for maintaining on the Judgments Board a condensed report of exile warrants, pardons due and political bars, including the date, length, reason and sanctioning official.

#### 1.3.3) Applications Lists

Burgesses are responsible for maintaining on the Demagogue Board a condensed report of the outcome of all applications to hold citizenship or office, including the name of the application, nature of the application, result of the vote and date of permission or eligibility for reapplication

#### 1.3.4) Inappropriate Names Reports

Demagogues are responsible for maintaining on the Demagogues Board a condensed report of investigations in terms of the Commonwealth Preservation Act, which contains the relevant protocol.

### 1.4) Protocol for Political Sponsoring

Political sponsors vouch for the suitability for office of those they sponsor, and share responsibility for their actions. The sponsor of an official who contravenes the law (or an official who sponsors in contravention of the law) will be punished in terms of section 2.1.1 of the Integrity Assurance Act.

### 1.5) Admission of Citizens

See the Commonwealth Preservation Act.

### 1.6) Board Monitoring

See the Board Usage Guidelines.

### 1.7) Notification

Rucesion Officials are required to make report of uses of official action.

#### 1.7.1) Civic Actions

Admission of new citizens should only be reported on the Political Discourse Board as required by the Commonwealth Preservation Act. Acts of support, endow, sponsoring, widdling, and renouncing citizenship are exempt from notification. Any official who remove a legend of citizenship from an Aisling must document this act on the Justice Cases board.

#### 1.7.2) Political Attacks

Notice of removal, dismissal (by a sponsor) or demotion (by a superior) of an official must be posted on the Justice Board, including the reason and name of the assistant. Included in the notice should be copies of reports of relevant action taken or warranted against the citizen.

#### 1.7.3) Rucesion Execution

Reports of exiles, pardons of exile, political bars, or pardons of political bars must be posted to the Judgments Board, including the name of the assistant. In the case of an exile, a copy must be mailed to the exiled/barred aisling. Included in the notice should be copies of reports of relevant action taken against the aisling. If no report is posted within one Temuairan day of an exile or political bar, it will be deemed to be illegal.

#### 1.7.4) Rucesion Law

Formal warnings, banishments and forgiveness of banishments must be notified on the Guards Board. The report takes the form specified by the Guard Captains and/or Judges. On completion of the action, a copy of the report is to be mailed to the offender(s) by the official making the final report. Reports are not to be sent to banished Fragile Chrysalis. If no report is posted within one Temuairan day of a banishment, it will be deemed to be illegal. Guards and guard captains are also required to send evidence of their cases within one Temuairan day to all Judicial administrators; failure to do so will result in a demerit.

#### 1.7.5) Other Actions

Notice of panel requests, panel results, summons to court, and notice of trial must be placed on the Judgment Board, and a copy mailed to the Aisling(s) involved. Notification of taking office is not required except as required by the Commonwealth Preservation Act. Notice of Demerit must be placed on the Justice Cases board.

### 1.8) Heresy Prevention

Heresy is described in the Standards of Practice. It is the duty of all officials to warn for heresy harassment. Any Official who fails to do so will face an infraction as per the Integrity Assurance Act.

### 1.9) Personal Involvement

Officials need to retain objectivity at all times so cognizance is taken of possible involvement.

#### 1.9.1) Direct Involvement

In any situation in which an official is a victim or alleged offender in a situation, that Official may not act on behalf of Rucesion, but instead must seek another qualified Official to handle the situation.

#### 1.9.2) Emotional Involvement

In any situation in which an Official is involved in a situation where either a victim or alleged offender in a situation is a family member, spouse, fae-bonded love or guildmate of an Official, the Official to seek the assistance of another Official to deal with the situation. If no other official is awake and to offender is a threat to Rucesion or her citizens, the involved Official may take only the action required to dispel the threat. In other cases, the evidence is to be lodged with a superior or peer to warrant appropriate action. If a second Official is called and the offender then attempts to involve him also, that Official may take immediate action against the offender.

## 2) Protocol for the Judicial Branch

This Section relates to the responsibilities of Guards, Captains and Judges. Reporting protocols are governed by Section 4 of the Notification Act.

### 2.1) Definitions

#### 2.1.1) Informal Warning

An informal warning consists of explaining to the offender that he is violating Rucesion law, and requesting that the activity cease.

#### 2.1.2) Formal Warning

A formal warning is posted to the Justice Cases Board and a copy thereof sent to the offender.

#### 2.1.3) Banishment

Any act of Banishment removes an offender from Rucesion soil for eight days.

##### 2.1.3.1) Banishment Warrant

A banishment warrant is an instruction to all Enforcers to banish an Aisling on sight.

##### 2.1.3.2) Forgive Banishment

If evidence and circumstances warrant it, an Enforcer may forgive the banishment of an Aisling.

### 2.2) Protocol for Criminal Offences

The duty of a Guard is to keep the peace, which is done with a helpful and conciliatory attitude, retaining objectivity and keeping a level head. Attempts should be made to defuse situations rather than polarize adversaries. Informal warnings should be given as explanations or reminders, not as threats. Only if the informal warning is unsuccessful or the protocol for the particular crime precludes it, should further action be taken. Guards should wear their uniforms whenever practical; to remain visible to the citizens they serve.

#### 2.2.1) Misdemeanors

Misdemeanors should be given a formal warning before an act of banishment is taken. A Judge, Guard Captain or two Guards may issue banishment without warning, at their discretion.

#### 2.2.2) Felonies

Any Felony offences incur instant banishment; thereafter the law enforcement official is to lodge evidence with a superior. If sufficient evidence exists, a felony offence may also be warranted for an exile of at least one deoch.

#### 2.2.3) Capital Crimes

Any Capital Crime requires that the law enforcement official view sufficient evidence to indicate the nature of the crime and identity of the criminal before banishing. Any additional evidence and testimony is then to be collected and lodged with a superior so that further action, as appropriate for the severity of the crime may be taken as needed.

### 2.3) Protocol for Banishment Warrants

Banishment warrants, in the form of a full report, are placed on the Justice Cases Board when a law enforcement official is unable to execute banishment.

### 2.4) Protocol for filling banishment warrants

After fulfilling a banishment warrant, or banishment request from a board monitoring official, the banishing official is to post a banishment report to the Justice Cases Board, to which is appended a copy of the warrant or banishment request, which is then to be removed from the board by a Captain. In the case of a wrongful banishment in fulfillment of a banishment warrant, the responsibility for the action is placed on the official who initiated the warrant.

### 2.5) Protocol for forgiveness of banishment

Forgiveness of banishment can only take place in the light of evidence of improper banishment and can be warranted only by the guard who carried out the original banishment, or a Captain.

### 2.6) Protocol for Exile Requests and Warrants

The Commonwealth of Rucesion reserves the right to exile criminals from its territory for crimes against its citizens. Exile requests are placed on the Judgments Board by Captains, who monitor the Justice Cases Board to detect habitual criminals. The Court of Rucesion may warrant exiles in its discretion, including in response to exile requests, for various crimes, or as a judgment from a trial. Such warrants are to indicate the length of the exile.

The Legislative Branch is free to execute warranted exiles at will. If a Demagogue cannot be located to fill an exile warrant against an Aisling in Rucesion territory, he is to be banished. After an exile is fulfilled, the warrant, of which a copy is to be attached to the exiling legislator's post, is to be removed by a judge. Memories are unnecessary in this case.

#### 2.6.1) Standard Exiles

A standard exile may be no less than one and no more than two deochs per charge.

### 2.7) Protocol for policing Fragile Chrysalis

Officials may take punitive action against a Fragile Chrysalis who commits crime against the Commonwealth or her Citizens, as dictated by Rucesion Law, or against any Fragile Chrysalis with a profane name.

## 3) Protocol for the Legislative Branch

This Section refers to the responsibilities of Demagogues and Burgesses. Reporting protocol is governed by Section 3 of the Notification Act.

### 3.1) Protocol for Exiles and Pardons

The Commonwealth of Rucesion reserves the right to exile criminals from its territory for crimes against its citizens.

#### 3.1.1) Exile Procedures

The Demagoguery executes exiles warranted by the Court. After the end of the exile period, a pardon may be executed by the Demagoguery on request of the exilee. A Demagogue who exiles an aisling without a warrant will face an infraction as per the Integrity Assurance Act.

#### 3.1.2) Pardon Procedures

An official who pardons an exile or lifts a political bar before due date is to receive an infraction as per the Removal from Office Procedures, this includes the official or citizen who assisted the pardon. A Burgess or Judge may issue a new warrant for the remaining days of the original exile.

### 3.2) Protocol for Political Bars

Political Bars, which are applied and removed by Burgesses, are classified in two categories. If additional contraventions occur during the period of the bar, an extension may be warranted jointly by a Judge and Burgess. Only one official substitution is permitted in this instance. Political Bars that are not officially enacted can be considered served after a period of eight Deochs, provided the individual does not seek office during that time. Any Aisling who served a Political Bar is considered to be on Probation for the next three Political terms they serve, above the office of Respected Citizen.

#### 3.2.1) Standard Political Bar

A Standard Political Bar is defined as any political bar lasting from one to five Deochs in length. Once the bar expires, if no additional crimes or circumstances which would warrant extension have occurred, the bar may be removed.

#### 3.2.2) Semi-Permanent Political Bar

A Semi-Permanent Political Bar is defined as any political bar lasting for a term of six to eight Deochs in length. Once the term has expired, the individual may be reviewed for signs of rehabilitation in meeting with a Judge and one other Rucesion Official, Knight or Ranger. At this point, the Officials must agree to remove the bar, or to extend it for a period of one Deoch. If an extension is issued, the individual will be reviewed again, following the same process, at the end of one Deoch.

### 3.3) Protocol for Law Instatement

Any proposed legislation is discussed at a meeting of the Legislature. The senior member of the Legislature is to open the voting upon reasonable discussion of the measure among the Legislature.

#### 3.3.1) Voting

To revise, instate, or repeal a law, two-thirds of the votes cast must show support for the proposal. No law may be passed with less than three Legislators casting their votes on the proposal. In the instance of multiple proposals, votes must be cast separately for each. All Legislators must cast their vote(s) for the proposal(s) within one moon of the opening, or indicate abstention. Once a moon has passed, the senior member of the Legislature is to close the vote, count, and post the results to the Demagogue's Board.

#### 3.3.2) Law Posting

No vote is required to remove a law replaced by a revision. A Burgess or Senior Demagogue is to post the new law, including the date the law was passed and all officials who voted on it.

Any Legislator who places an article on the Law Board without following these procedures is to be removed from office and barred for one Deoch.

#### 3.3.3) Amendments to the Charter

Any attempt to amend the Charter of Rucesion follows section 3.3.1 of this law, except as follows: The voting period is extended to a double moon; All Senior Officials must vote or place notice of abstention on the Political Discourse Board; The proposal must attain 80% approval to be passed. Any Amendment to the Rucesion Charter is to be posted separately from the Charter. The Rucesion Charter may only be removed by a 95% approval of all Senior Officials

### 3.4) Protocol for Inappropriate Name Investigations

The Demagoguery maintains the integrity of the Commonwealth concerning the names of Rucesion citizens in terms of the Inappropriate Names Law.

## 4) Protocol for Ranger and Knight Interaction

All officials of Rucesion are required to assist any Knight or Ranger in any way possible, except in the event that the safety and/or integrity of the Commonwealth, it's practices, or standards are placed in danger. In such an event, all officials of Rucesion are expected to contact another Ranger or Knight to investigate the situation, and clarify the level of cooperation needed and expected.

## 5) Rights of the Accused

Aislings who are punished under the Rucesion Law are entitled to full knowledge of the nature of their case, including evidence, names and testimonies of witnesses, and the place and nature of their crime. They are also entitled to know where they may appeal their case if they so request, and officials who are asked this must refer to their superior(s) in the chain in the command (Judges should refer to another Judge or a Knight.) These rights are waived where the accused fails to request this information in a civil manner, where these rights would clash with a witness' insistence for confidentiality, or where the accused is a Fragile Chrysalis.
