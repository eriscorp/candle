# Trial Procedures Law

The purpose of trials is to resolve disputes that cannot otherwise be put to rest, and which are not fully provided for by the auspices of the rest of Rucesion Law. A trial should not be used to contest a case that is already covered in full by Rucesion Law, a Judge or Burgess' findings, or a panel committee's verdicts. Where insufficient evidence for a case exists, but witnesses do, and the Judge considers the case to have merit, a trial may be held.

## 1) Requesting a Trial

If an Aisling feels the need to seek a trial, they should contact the Judge who currently holds office, in writing. This written document should contain all the information pertaining to the alleged crime: when and where the alleged crime took place, and the names of all parties involved, including all witnesses.

It is then left to the discretion of the Judge to determine if this case warrants a trial, or if the matter can or should be settled out of court.

## 2) Trial Investigation

Upon receipt of a request for trial the presiding Judge should review the material presented to them as soon as possible. Each Aisling involved should be contacted and asked to provide a written testimony to the court immediately. A prosecutor should be appointed by the Judge to aid in the investigation of the pending case, and provide a second perspective on the matter. The Aisling chosen to hold this position should be experienced in the execution of the law, having held office in Rucesion and thought to possess the ability to exercise jurisprudence.

## 3) Trial Schedule

The Judge must set a tentative date and mail it to the plaintiff and the defendant. If either party finds that the proposed date is unacceptable to them they must inform the court in a timely manner, with good reason, in writing.

### 3.1) Trial in Absentia

If, after significant consideration to the defendant/plaintiffs schedule, a date cannot be agreed upon then the court will be forced to set a time and date and demand that the plaintiff/defendant are in attendance. If either party fails to be present at the scheduled proceedings, without good reason, they forfeit their rights to said trial, i.e. plaintiff's complaint will be dropped, and the defendant will be tried and sentenced at the court's discretion, in absentia.

If a witness cannot be in attendance their testimony can be presented to the court in written form. But if this witness is seen in Temuair at any time during the trial proceedings their testimony will be struck from the court's record, and they will be held in contempt of court for their failure to appear.

If either the plaintiff or defendant wishes to retain counsel they must inform the Judge and all communications henceforth will be made through said counsellor.

## 4) Judge and Jury

Before the actual proceedings begin the Judge will need to instruct the jury as to their duties and responsibilities. The jury must be informed that they are to judge the facts of the case in order to determine guilt or innocence, but the Judge is the sole arbitrer of the laws. The Judge will state to the jury the crime(s) for which the defendant is being tried and the laws in question will be read out loud. The jury will also be instructed that they are not, at any time, to speak out loud. ((The jury is to have 'Do Not Disturb' status and must be grouped with the judge, who will monitor their deliberations. If any member of the jury has any question or desires clarification of any point, they are to group whisper the Judge. ))

### 4.1) Contempt of Court - Jury

The jury should be instructed as to the importance of their role in these proceedings and warned that if they do not comply with the court they risk being held in contempt, and charged with obstruction of justice. All present in the courtroom should also be instructed that there is to be no speaking out of turn as they also can be held in contempt of court and dealt with accordingly. First a warning, second removal from the courthouse, and thirdly banishment from the village in which the trial is being held. If this still does not subdue the troublemaker an exile should be considered.

## 5) Trial Proceedings

Once all participants are in place and have received instruction the trial shall commence.

The prosecutor shall present a brief opening statement to the court to explain the nature of the case and mention what is to be proven. This should summarily introduce the case. Then it will be the defendant's turn. This chronological order will persist throughout trial. At any time during the proceedings, either party, or the Judge, can raise objections. Otherwise there is to be complete silence. The prosecutor will then present his case by calling witnesses and disclosing evidence. The witness will be directly examined by the side which summons them, and the opposing side will have the opportunity to cross-examine the witness. Then a redirect will be possible. After both sides present their cases, they may conclude with closing arguments.

## 6) Verdict

After closing arguments the jury may now begin deliberation as a group. Once each juror feels he or she has come to a decision they may exit the jury box. Upon doing so they will enter their vote of innocence, guilt or indecision. The judge gathers and tallies all votes. If the voting were to result in a tie it would be up to the presiding Judge to cast the deciding vote. If the majority vote is innocent, the defendant is released immediately. If the majority vote is guilty the Judge must then pass sentence on the defendant as outlined by the law in question.

## 7) Trial Minutes

A brief summary of all court proceedings must be posted to the Judgements board as soon as possible.

**7.1)** Mistrial - a mistrial may be declared by any other presiding judge or by a panel of two Guard Captains if no other judge is presently sitting on the bench. Mistrials shall consist of any of the following:

- Trials not held in accordance with the trial procedures law.
- Verdicts awarded that are not provisions under Rucesion law (where applicable and an exact clause for said crime exists under Rucesion Law)
- Jury Tampering

A finding that a mistrial has occurred may completely overrule the verdict of guilty in the aforementioned trial as well as any punishment awarded, only in the case of jury tampering. In all other cases, a second trial is to be held, including all innocent verdicts.

### 7.2) Safeguarding of Objectivity

To prevent Jury tampering and ensure that all deliberations are public record during a trial, the jury will have their whispers off as will the Judge. All arguments will be kept in the open with one exception: both parties will be able to request private conference with the Judge, which is to be done in writing ((by mail)) and the Judge is responsible for keeping memories of such parchments. Juries shall hear only the evidence presented within the court. Requests for clarification of testimony or evidence shall be up to the discretion of the presiding Judge. Jurors will make their ruling based only upon evidence presented as it is presented.

### 7.3) Waiver of Trial by Jury

A defendant may forego their right to a trial by jury by forwarding their consent to the presiding judge in writing. This agreement will be entered into the official court record and confirmed verbally prior to opening argument. This shall be the only instance in which a criminal trial will be conducted without the required jury.
