# Weak Sparks or Weak Perception?

_by Miabi in **Dark Ages**_

Over the course of my time as a priest of Glioca, I have, as have many others,
had to cope with those aislings whom some would claim have a weak or dim
spark. In the course of my observations, however, it has occurred to me that
this is not anywhere near an accurate depiction of these aislings. The idea
that is so often touted is that these aislings simply lack creativity because
their spark is weak and so they don't see the lands in the same way as some
others. On more careful observation, however, we see that these aislings can
be and often are quite creative in pursuing their various ends. Indeed, they
only seem to have trouble perceiving the world around them, not in creatively
coping with what they perceive.

In fact, the perception of these aislings seems to be quite limited. They seem
to see only a shadow of the world. Actually, the descriptions some use remind
me of the tiny sculpted figures one of the boys near my home had when I was
growing up. He would take out these tiny metal figures and set them up on the
path near his home, setting them up on the road in battle formations and then
pretending that these little figures of his actually went to battle with each
other. As a young girl, I thought this rather silly, but the boy seemed to
fantasize about this in much the same way as I fantasized about being a mother
to my baby doll. In both cases, the inanimate objects did nothing on their
own. When I imagined the doll cried, it did not really cry. When the boy
imagined one of his tiny figures fighting, it did not really swing its sword
and no one really died. The actual worlds of the figurines and my doll were
quite limited, but in our imagination they took on a life of their own, with a
detailed and colorful world. The actual representation was just a shadow or,
perhaps more accurately, a model of the world in our imaginations. That seems
to be how some aislings perceive the real world as well. Due to some strange
sickness or condition they see only the barest model of the actual world. I
purposely switched from saying "perceive" to "see" in describing this because
they do not even seem to be able to perceive with all of their senses.

Even the senses these aislings do seem to possess would seem to be quite
limited. They do not see the sun rise or the sun set for instance (which also
seems to interfere with their sense of the passage of time). They also do not
seem aware of the pain of the creatures they slay, the pain of their comrades,
or, remarkably, even their own, perceiving only instead whether each is weak
or strong still. This last baffles me, but it seems to be true. Of course, the
area where I come into contact with them the most is in the temple. There they
seem unaware of Glioca's presence or the presence of any of the other gods
anywhere in Temuair. I suppose the concept of a god is too much for their
limited model of the world, the framework by which they seem to try to
understand it. They seem unable to conceive of anything beyond the purely
physical and often seem to have trouble even with that. Their concept of the
gods seems limited to the powers that gods grant their followers.

Actually, in talking about the powers that gods grant their followers, it is
interesting to note how these aislings try to interact with the world around
them. Their simple model of the world seems to incorporate only elements of
raw power. So, naturally, this seems to be what most of them pursue. The types
of power they understand seem limited to the powers gained with insight, the
power of rank in the temple, and the power of rank in politics. Their models
do not seem to include any of the reasons behind these powers, however, and so
it is natural that they often abuse these powers.

They do have a social structure, however. In fact, they seem keenly aware of
the power such structures can give them. Many are able to communicate as
effectively as any of the supposedly brighter aislings. Though they do often
shorten and slur their speech. I am unsure of the exact reason for this, but
the primary reason seems to be some form of laziness or hurriedness. Despite
this, they seem to have little trouble understanding each other and most
aislings have come to understand this slurred speech, despite its
distastefulness to some. Consequently, they are able to perceive what others
are saying to them and to communicate their own thoughts as well. Of course,
they have no framework for understanding what some of us tell them, for their
model of the world seems only to include raw power as mentioned before.
Speaking of the gods, therefore, is still gibberish to them, even though they
understand the words. Very often the only thing they respect is raw power and
their social groups are largely organized around this simple understanding.
Some would call these friendships, but I think that's a misunderstanding of
what it is to be a friend. They do, however, often form true friendships as
well as making allies to gain power. In any case, beyond a few friends and
allies that they make, they seem to be unable to truly understand or
participate in a larger community structure, since that requires a more
elaborate model of the world around them.

In talking about communication, I could also discuss heresy, of course, but
doing so is rather pointless. In short, though, they seem to have a shared
delusion of another world that they seem to have no trouble perceiving and
often, to their shame, discuss in public. I find it sad that they have such a
rich perceptive model of a false world and such a weak one of the real world.

Now some will be wondering if its possible to cure or help such aislings. In
my observations, I have come to the conclusion that it is only possible to do
so if they are willing to help themselves. Indeed, this is the one area where
perhaps saying that they have a weak spark is most accurate, because it does
not seem to illuminate their minds so that they may have an accurate model of
the world around them. Still, I would shy away from saying their spark is weak
so much as saying it is unused. That is, it has the power to illuminate their
minds, but they are not aware of or choose not to use that particular power.
In any case, to be cured, they must use their spark to build a better
perceptive model of the world, if you will. If they do, then we can help them
to build it, but we can not force them to undertake such a task in the first
place. If they do not do this themselves, as most never seem to, then there is
nothing we can really do.

So, lastly I suppose I should mention how to cope with such aislings, since
few will ever undertake to cure themselves. As a priestess of Glioca, my
answer is, of course, to deal with them in compassion. After all, how can one
not feel compassion for the pour soul that can not feel the touch of a lover,
see the beauty of a sunset, or know the peace of worshipping a god? Of course,
I'm fond of saying that just because I have compassion on the wolf, doesn't
mean I let him in with the sheep. The wolf is not really evil, merely dealing
with the world according to what he perceives, much like these aislings are.

May the gods all give us all the strength to endure it.

_Penned by Miabi, a Priestess Deacon of Glioca_  
_Submitted by Miabi as her entry in the Philosophy Contest_ `(( December 10, 2004 ))`
