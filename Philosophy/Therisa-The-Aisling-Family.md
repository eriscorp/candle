# The Aisling Family

_by Therisa in **Dark Ages**_

## A Look At The Aisling Family

The familial structure of Aislings is unique in that families are created
through mutual agreement between two or more Aislings. When one becomes an
Aisling, his or her past life as a Mundane becomes a vague memory. Whatever
family one had as a Mundane is usually forgotten upon obtaining Aislinghood.
While an Aisling might claim to recognize another Aisling as a family member
from their Mundane past, it may not be factual, but instead a feeling of
kinship that makes them believe they were once related.

Aisling form families through marriage/glading, adoption or bonding (which
involves brothers, sisters, cousins, etc.). An Aisling may also acquire more
relatives if a current family member of his or hers takes on a new
relationship, such as acquiring a brother through his or her parents adopting
another Aisling.

Marriage (or in the case of two same gender Aislings, glading) joins two
Aislings together as spouses. Any relative in each Aisling's family now
technically becomes an in-law to each of the partners. A joined couple may
wish to be parents, although a single Aisling may also choose to become a
mother or father. This is done through adoption, as there is no evidence of
Aislings coming into existence through reproductive means. Adoption is a
mutual agreement between the parent or parents and the Aisling to be adopted.
Although the family registry of an Aisling allows for the name of only one
child to be recorded, an Aisling parent may adopt as many children as he or
she wishes.

Relations such as brothers and sisters can occur either independently of
parents or through being adopted into a family with already established
children. If two Aislings want to bond as siblings, they can establish this
relationship on their own. However, most such relationships usually occur
through being adopted into a family and accepting those family members as
one's own.

It is of interest to note that an Aisling's deoch of birth has little to do
with what familial position they hold in relation to another family member. A
parent may very well be several deoches younger than his or her child.
Experience of the world and of one's path seems to bear more weight on the
choosing of family. For example, an Aisling well versed in his or her path
will usually look for an Aisling new to that path in order to teach and guide
them, as well as provide support and love.

The matter of extended families is usually friendly and well received among
Aislings. If an Aisling adopts another Aisling who has an already established
brother or sister relation, the adoptive parent will most likely accept this
sibling as his or her own child. Of course, the parent or other child may
choose not to recognize this new member as being a part of their family, since
they did not make the agreement between themselves from the start. Also, when
two Aislings establish a relationship such as siblings, they may already each
have parents of their own. Either these relationships may be kept separate, or
an Aisling may refer to each set of parents as being his or her own, allowing
for an Aisling to have one or more mothers and fathers.

Aisling familial relationships are very complex, yet easy to form. Without
blood ties, Aislings may make a family with whomever they are compatible with
and feel close to, allowing for such unique characteristics as multiple
parents. A downside to this is that relationships are just as easy to break,
and once an Aisling is dismissed from a family relationship, one may never
know that he or she had ever been there.

_written by: Therisa_
