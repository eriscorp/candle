# Way of the Sea

_by Saki in **Dark Ages**_

## A Sailor's Philosophy

So you want to know the slaves of the cold mistress? Every steely eyed Aisling
and Mundane that can chew iron and spit out ship bolts! However, they are not
all the same, nor what motivates them. Most importantly, why do they cling to
their lives at sea? I will attempt to categorize the different types of
sailors and their creeds and theorize on how these arch types came to the
sailing way. There is also the question of how the many great superstitions
and stories are formed and implemented, which I will attempt to prove actually
helps keep the ships sailing. The taverns and inns, ports, and decks of
clippers were backdrop to the eventual realization that to explain the
Sailor's Philosophy lies within the theory I have dubbed 'Way of the Sea'
comprised of unspoken, unwritten truths and glories.

While to most unknowing folks all sailors are the same; this couldn't be
further from the truth. I believe, while no generalization is true, that there
are nine key arch types of the sea breed. I theorize that they are separated
geographical orientation (home port), social status, and traditions. They
include:

**The Oren Marinaio**: The Oren Marinaio, or sailing man has typically a more
dusky skin coloration, which I believe is due to constant tropical exposure,
compared to most of Ardmagh. In times long gone, Oren Marinaio were recruited
into their own small navy, until the port's decline with the absence of King
Oren. Now it would seem they are employed directly into the lower social set
of sailing crews, as they are most notoriously known as pirates, despite
having a large contingent of law abiding seamen. The most notable Marinaio
tradition is the wearing of bright and festive clothing, pirate or not. A
bright red, blue, orange cap or bandana, a brightly (and very often striped)
shirt, and flowing pantaloons would be the best bet to blending in at the Oren
docks. The Marinaio is the source of the stereotypical pirate accent; however
the common sailor seems to share a more northern accent, if having one at all.
Marinaio that are very proud of Oren are most likely pirates. A common saying
among the Marinaio is "A murky sea means you can't see the shark that gets
you."

**The Abel Stigandr (wanderer)**: The most northern and most active sea port
in terms of Mundanes and Aisling combined, Abel is the key to the export of
the Suomian valley, and is home port for the majority of long range cold
weather vessels. I believe this has impacted the Stigandr greatly. The look of
the Stigandr is a strapping large man and a fair haired maiden. Many of the
finest sailors (even in the Loures Navy) call Abel their home. Despite having
such a dialect that is the most notorious of all when the Stigandr speaks
common. A very tradition steeped sailor-type, the Stigandr is especially
superstitious of 'spirits' and often carve magic runes of the Abel language
into the masts and other parts of their ships. A saying heard often, though I
believe to have Milethan influence is 'The sun never shines on closed eyes."

**Undine Valley Fishers**: This small group of mundanes ensures that there are
enough food stores for their family and the towns of Suomi and Undine. They
commonly salt and spice their extra catch, and trade them with Stigandr who
sell them as a delicacy to Rucesion elite. There is no social class for these
fishers, and, while resembling the northern arch type of sailor, are almost
never seen outside of the valley's waters, due to the simple fact they are
happy there, and it seems they have little or no desire to leave. Their
superstitious nature only seems to revolve around how many fish they can or
cannot catch.

**Smugglers**: Of all shapes and sizes, the smuggler comes from no port in
particular, but has a definite center in Abel and Rucesion. The existence of
the black markets relies heavily upon the sailor who's willing to trick and
sneak their way through patrol searches and local tariffs, reaping the profits
of extremely inflated prices. They are commonly referred to as tooth-scratches
and do their best to blend in as normal merchant ships. They are a step or two
above pirates, since their existence means select pockets will remain padded.

**Pirates**: The absolute lowest social order of sailor is the pirate. While
some have strict codes and ethics, they are all lumped mostly into one band of
murderous, greedy, blood-thirsty individuals who care for none and want for
themselves. Almost every active port (and some covert bases) have a pirate
presence, and they are often involved in street fights with Loures Seamen.
How each pirate Captain gets their ship is unknown, normally it's never
probably legal.

**The Loures Navy and Merchant Marin**: The Marin is outwardly in line with
the occupants of Rucesion and the Loures proper area including Piet. Of the
self-imposed social order of sailors, Marin are the highest, and normally
makes that quite clear at every opportunity. Marin are very commonly Captains
and high ranking officers or officials on whichever boat they see, often
coming from upper and middle class families. These are the most commonly
sociably 'acceptable' sailors, and the ones that are over-romanticized. They
police and protect interests of King Bruce and the Loures court, no matter
their vessel, which is why I attribute their social class as highest through
Loures association.

**Medenian Natives**: While having their own unique set of superstitions, most
trace back to the only explored ruins in the Noes region of Medenia,
prophecies and accounts of the spirits. These individuals I place as no social
standing, rather as an old set of sailors who provide both food, and trade
connections throughout the Medenian continent. The extent of which has yet to
be determined, something I am researching actively. I have ascertained that
this unique arch type of sailor still developed in similar enough way to be
included in the Way of the Sea. Though, any alcoholic drinks they have aren't
as favored by other arch types, due to the fact that few sailors from Ardmagh
can stand how sweet the common Medenian drink is. The thoughts of a Medenian
native are central to their village, which are larger than Suomi or Undine,
caravans, and tribes being the exception.

While different elements drift them apart, I believe the sea works in its own
way and brings even conflicting and different arch-types together, from the
alehouse up the gangplank and on an oversea adventure. (Viking) One of the
ways this manifests, I believe is how so many stories and superstitions are
common, no matter the arch type, among all sailors. Sitting over tepid ale the
unshaven Stigandr leans in to the gathered Marin, who are watching him
intently. It is at this point obvious they are brought together by the tale
of a great storm, the Stigandr claims was caused by the angry wills of the
ocean for the Captain of his Loures vessel not to have placed a figurehead on
the bow. Tales and superstitions are religiously uniform as they are taken
seriously, and I believe this is because the Way of the Sea binds them
together. The core of these tales and glories all lie in the theorized,
unspoken, Way of the Sea.

Now, in detail, will be described the Way of the Sea, which I theorize is the
reason and willpower that goes unspoken in sending and keeping the sea lanes
free and blue. It will begin with several truths which are:

- Always be true to your ship, and it will be true to you.
- Follow the tradition of your ancestors, or face the wrath of an angry sea.
- Every ship needs a home port.
- Song and tale as the anchor chain.
- Seek what's going on in your sea, your Temuair.
- Respect the sea and avoid the tempest.
- Land matters are land matters. Sea matters are sea matters.
- If you have a partner in every port, never go on holiday.
- The best way to greet new folks is with alcohol.
- The best way to greet enemies is with a cannon and sword.
- Never rat on a fellow sailor if you're just as guilty.
- The sea is a cold mistress, we are here at her mercy to find our purpose
  and way.

In simplicity of these truths belie the Way of the Sea, they flow and
interact, and I theorize that in the will of this way, all sailors set their
sights on the horizon, whether it's a battered woman, a cuckolded and now
homeless man, a noble, the peasant, the Aisling, the Mundane. The way of the
sea is in every arch type, every story, every whisper and breath of every
salty horse to ride the azure mists in their soul. It is the pursuit of self
and fellowship that sets these truths, while never spoken or written until
now, in the hearts of sailing-kind. This is, I believe, how they all endure
the unpredictable sea, miserable sailing conditions, stale food, separation,
death, sickness, and no guarantee of lively hood, providing life-blood and
commerce to all peoples.

_May the wind always be at your back,_  
_Saki of Abel_

Dictated by Saki, scribed at the hand of Runa of the Wandering Mist Inn, Abel.

## Appendix: Understanding the Abel Accent in Common

The difference in the old language of Abel is often shown through the accent
obtained when a resident of this area speaks common. Most commonly small words
are shortened to the sound of one letter, and must be extrapolated by the
listener. With these basic guides, mastery of comprehension is a simple task.
`((Think of the sound as a Scandinavian/Icelandic accent in English.))`

Aye- Yes  
Nae- No  
Ahoy!- Hey you over there!  
Yer – Your, you're, you are  
Git – Get  
Dun- Don't, done  
Dunchu- Don't you  
Hail- Good sun!  
Tha- The  
Loard- Lord (Tha loard a' Abel, Fiosachd!)  
A'- of  
Arit- All right  
Cours- Of course  
Dacha- Outsider (A word taken from artic nomads.) `((Sami))`
